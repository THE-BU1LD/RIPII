ABSTRACT
A physics-structured predictor can outperform a generic baseline without demonstrating that its
adaptive component is useful. We examine this distinction for the RIPII radial-mixture model, which
combines two history-conditioned central-force experts using a bounded scene-dependent gate. Ear-
lier development results remain inconclusive because training adequacy was not established. We
specify a new comparison against independently trained ﬁxed gating, graph, pooling, and Trans-
former predictors, together with a within-checkpoint gate intervention. The protocol requires six
independent data-generation blocks per domain, two nested initialization seeds, validation-only hy-
perparameter selection, and explicit training-adequacy checks before test access. Its implementation
passed ten original CPU numerical tests and full-horizon CUDA backward and checkpoint-replay
checks for all ﬁve models. A measured cost proﬁle then rejected the 140-ﬁt matrix under the au-
thorized four allocated GPU-hour budget. No comparison training or test evaluation occurred. We
report the model, mathematical guarantees and limitations, protocol, retained failure evidence, and
an engineering investigation of execution overhead. These results establish an executable speciﬁca-
tion and a concrete feasibility limitation, not a positive scientiﬁc result for adaptive gating.

Introduction
The research question is whether scene-dependent mixing of two radial force experts improves held-out trajectory
prediction beyond the same radial architecture with a ﬁxed mixture. The distinction matters because geometric
structure, integration rules, information access, and optimization can each improve a model independently of
adaptation. A comparison against a generic graph predictor alone cannot identify the adaptive contribution.
The working hypothesis is precise: under the declared synthetic springs and charged-particle distributions, adequately
trained adaptive radial prediction reduces mean paired 64-step position error by at least two percent relative to
independently trained ﬁxed radial prediction in both domains. A stronger mechanism claim additionally requires an
advantage over replacing each trained adaptive gate by its training-set mean. This claim is falsiﬁable by adequately
trained controls that match or outperform the adaptive predictor, by inadequate precision, or by failure of the
intervention requirement.
The prior radial-mixture v3 study is retained as INCONCLUSIVE_UNDERTRAINED. Its descriptive rankings are not
converted into conﬁrmatory evidence. The new study is a separate identity, RIPII-RADIAL-ADAPTATION-v1, with
fresh generator blocks and a protected evaluation boundary. Its question remains unresolved because measured
execution cost prevented training. This report does not claim perfection, state-of-the-art performance, real-world
utility, or a new physical law.

Related Work and Contribution Boundary
Interaction Networks explicitly model objects and relations for dynamical prediction [1]. NRI jointly infers latent
interactions and predicts dynamics from observations [2]; the present experiments use its retained springs and charged-
particle simulator implementation, not a reproduction of published NRI scores. EGNNs provide an established route
to geometric equivariance [3]. These precedents rule out describing relational modeling or invariant-to-vector updates
as new by themselves.
NIIP instead represents relational constraints through learned energy functions and test-time trajectory optimization
[4]. Our direct autoregressive predictor does not implement that inference mechanism. In particular, a velocity-
dependent central force should not be described as an energy-conserving potential model without an additional
derivation.
The contribution pursued here is a controlled test of adaptation within a speciﬁed radial predictor. The listed baselines
are strong matched controls from this project’s history, not a comprehensive current state-of-the-art suite. NRI,
EGNN, potential-based methods, and task-speciﬁc analytic estimators remain relevant external comparisons before a
broad superiority claim. No priority claim for the two-expert gate is made.
CUDA graph execution can reduce repeated host dispatch overhead for static workloads [5]. Its applicability must be
measured: a software optimization is neither a novel dynamics model nor evidence that the scientiﬁc hypothesis is
true.

Integration, initialization, and complexity
The update is semi-implicit Euler:
vt+1,i = vt,i + ∆t at,i,
pt+1,i = pt,i + ∆t vt+1,i.
(6)
The output linear layers of both experts and the boundary head start at zero. Consequently the initial predictor is
constant velocity, while the gate is initially unidentiﬁable from prediction: both expert outputs coincide. Standard
module initialization is retained for the other layers. No auxiliary gate loss or supervised edge objective is used.
For width w, pair computation is O(BHN 2w2) and stored unrolled activations are O(BHN 2w) up to layer-
dependent constants. The implementation explicitly ﬁxes N = 5 through its receiver/sender buffers; variable-size
generalization is not demonstrated. The adaptive model contains 21,956 stored and gradient-active parameters. Fixed
radial stores the same number but has 17,475 gradient-active parameters in the qualiﬁcation check.

Controls and Identiﬁability
The primary comparator is a separately initialized and trained ﬁxed-radial model with the same two experts, history
encoders, boundary term, and integration rule. It isolates the trainable gate only imperfectly: the optimization path
and effective parameter count also differ.
A second comparison intervenes on the selected adaptive checkpoint. The replacement gate is its mean over training
scenes and forecast steps. Test observations never determine this constant. This intervention retains the trained experts
and removes scene-dependent gate variation, but can create an off-training-distribution internal representation. It is
therefore a diagnostic requirement in addition to, not a substitute for, independently trained ﬁxed gating.
The generic controls are the retained graph, global-pool, and Transformer implementations, each receiving the same
eight-frame state history and 64-step objective. Widths are chosen by the frozen search grid to minimize stored

Prospective Experimental Protocol
6.1
Data and protected boundary
The two domains are springs and charged particles from the pinned NRI simulator. The exact source and upstream
license are vendored in the retained package. Each ﬁt uses 512 training scenes and 128 validation scenes. Each test
regime would use 128 scenes. Sampling uses 50 simulator steps per observation and retains 72 observations. Every
saved scene must have the declared shape and ﬁnite values.
Calibration uses generator block 5100011 and initialization seed 61001. Main blocks are 5200011, 5300021,
5400031, 5500041, 5600051, and 5700061; nested initialization seeds are 62003 and 63001. A dataset seed is
100b + cd + cs + cr, where domain offsets are 0/50, split offsets are 1/2/3, and regime offsets are 0/10/20/30. This
makes the declared identities disjoint; it is not a proof of all possible forms of leakage.
The standard regime uses upstream defaults. Strong interaction uses strength 0.15 for springs and 1.5 for charged
particles; fast motion uses velocity norm 0.75; noisy observations use noise variance parameter 0.01. The original
simulator semantics govern these parameters. Regime changes are test-only. Test datasets may be generated only after
every selected main ﬁt passes adequacy and the selection manifest is frozen.
6.2
Training and adequacy
Two learning rates, 10−3 and 3 × 10−4, are calibrated independently per domain and model using eligible validation
performance. This gives 2 × 5 × 2 = 20 calibration ﬁts; the main matrix contains 2 × 6 × 2 × 5 = 120 ﬁts.
Training uses AdamW, weight decay 10−4, batch size 16, and global gradient-norm clipping at 1.0. A CPU generator
selects scenes and valid temporal windows. Curriculum horizons are 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32 for consecutive 500-update
stages, followed by horizon 64. Validation occurs every 500 updates using the full 64-step forecast. A plateau
scheduler multiplies the learning rate by 0.5 after patience 2, with minimum 3 × 10−6.
No ﬁt is eligible before 6,000 updates. The maximum is 16,000. At least ﬁve full-horizon validation records are
required. Both training and validation best-so-far position errors must improve by no more than one percent over
the ﬁnal four checks, and the selected checkpoint’s training position RMSE must be at most 75 percent of constant
velocity on the same training scenes. Only checkpoints trained at horizon 64 can be selected. The selected checkpoint
is reloaded and its metrics recomputed.
These conditions reject important forms of undertraining, but do not prove global optimality: a stalled poor optimizer
can plateau, and ﬁnite thresholds are conventions. Failure of any required ﬁt blocks test access. No failed seed is
silently replaced.
6.3
Inference and decision rules
For each domain and generator block, average error over the two nested initialization seeds before comparing
methods. The generator block, not a forecast frame or an optimization seed, is the independent unit. Deﬁne block
gain Gb = (Rb −Cb)/Rb and log error ratio Lb = log(Rb/Cb) for reference R and candidate C.
The one-sided paired sign-permutation test enumerates all 26 sign assignments to the six Lb values. This test relies
on the relevant exchangeability/symmetry assumption under its null; independent simulation blocks alone do not
prove that assumption. Holm adjustment covers the two primary domain comparisons. Adaptive beneﬁt requires
mean gain at least two percent, positive gain in every block, and adjusted p ≤0.05 in both domains. The mechanism
diagnostic also requires at least two percent mean gain and positive gains in every block against the training-mean
gate intervention.
Separate gates require at least ﬁve percent mean gain against each generic baseline, with every block positive, and no
more than ﬁve percent regression in any OOD block against the trained comparators. Passing the adaptive primary
gate does not automatically pass these stronger gates. Raw per-scene and per-horizon errors and predictions are
retained for replay. With six independent units, inference remains coarse; uncertainty must be interpreted within this
synthetic scope.

7.2
Execution optimization investigation
The engineering candidate removes repeated host-side tensor-value checks from the inner rollout, evaluates ﬁxed-
shape dense edge messages with explicit masks, and captures repeated CUDA training operations. Public entry checks
and post-update ﬁnite checks remain necessary; diagnostics can be computed using the original model outside the
timed training kernel. Dense masking must preserve the dynamic distance threshold and its gradient behavior.
Qualiﬁcation compares original and optimized outputs and parameter gradients at horizons 1, 8, and 64 after nonzero
head perturbations; zero-initialized heads alone would be a vacuous test. It additionally checks edge-threshold
cases, optimizer updates, serialization, invalid input rejection, and captured-versus-eager updates with changing
data. Predeclared numerical tolerances are absolute 2 × 10−5 and relative 2 × 10−4; checkpoint replay is exact. A
mismatch rejects equivalence rather than authorizing relaxed tolerances after inspection. The amended execution
candidate passed all ﬁve model ﬁxtures at the scientiﬁc weight decay 10−4, including a captured tensor learning-rate
change from 0.001 to 0.0005. The initial optimizer ﬁxture at default weight decay 0.01 is retained as a separate earlier
attempt.
The largest absolute parameter-gradient and ordinary optimizer-update differences were 2.38e-07 and 3.99e-07,
respectively. Captured-versus-eager optimizer updates and serialized forecast replay were exact in the tested cases.
These ﬁnite ﬁxtures are not a proof over every input or an independently reproduced training campaign.
The update-only minimum-step extrapolation is 13.10 host-hours, excluding data generation, validation, graph
capture, serialization, and safety reserve. This remains above the remaining authorized host time; the full study was
not admitted. All three numerical attempts together charge 0.5748 allocated GPU-hours, including export reserves,
with zero cloud spend.

Discussion and Limitations
This work has established neither that adaptation helps nor that it is useless. The new study has no test outcomes. The
earlier undertrained study cannot resolve the missing comparison, and a faster implementation cannot supply an effect
estimate by itself.
The next experiment is justiﬁed only if measured end-to-end cost ﬁts the remaining authorization and equivalence
checks pass. Otherwise the honest endpoint is an unresolved hypothesis with a documented feasibility limit. A
future narrower question can be worthwhile, but its claims must match its domains, baselines, and independent
units. Real-world performance, stability over longer horizons, object-count scaling, calibrated uncertainty, and
physical-parameter recovery remain untested.

Conclusion
A controlled adaptive-versus-ﬁxed comparison requires adequate training and a mechanism-speciﬁc intervention. We
implemented that comparison and veriﬁed its executable components, but measured cost prevented the experiment
under its declared budget. The hypothesis remains untested in the new study. The defensible result is the exact model
speciﬁcation, preserved evidence boundary, and measured feasibility failure, together with a separately qualiﬁed
attempt to reduce execution overhead. No unsupported positive conclusion is substituted for missing experiments.

Evidence and Acceptance Requirements
A valid efﬁcacy claim requires the chain: frozen protocol and source; complete calibration and main-ﬁt inventory;
passed adequacy records; frozen validation-only selections; raw protected predictions and targets; independent metric
and statistical replay; and ﬁgures/tables regenerated from those records. None of the engineering checks replaces the
missing trained-model portion of this chain.
The ﬁxed-radial comparison, within-checkpoint gate intervention, generic-baseline gate, and OOD gate must be
reported separately. A failure in one cannot be concealed by averaging it with a success in another. A missing
comparison is reported as missing, not zero improvement.
